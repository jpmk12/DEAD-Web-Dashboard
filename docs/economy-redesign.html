Review notes — what each part is for

  1. 1Reframed header + Δ line. The tab opens with who moved since your last look, in the same "net change, not a log" grammar as Glance. The old "Strategic Economics" title and the energy strip go; Brent lives inside corroboration now.
  2. 2Actor tiles. One per tracked actor (from Mission Profile AOIs + watched countries; non-state actors curated). Level, anomaly vs a 30-day baseline, trajectory, and instrument chips lit only by graded evidence. Same engine as the I&W boards, so learning mode caps at Watch and colour is earned.
  3. 3Coercion board. The unit is actor → target → instrument. Every row states its grade (act › threat › analysis), its evidence with source and age, what ships and prices did, and which of your lines it touches. A mention counter never appears on this tab.
  4. 4Evidence drawer. Tapping a row opens every source that fed it, each graded, plus the corroboration numbers and the judgement with its falsifier. "＋ log a call" writes to the same decision log the I&W boards use, so hit rates accrue here too.
  5. 5Chokepoint strip. The existing graded board compressed to one row with a 7-day AIS sparkline per strait; detail is one tap away. Suppressed traffic is the one sensor that cannot be spun.
  6. 6Leverage map. Structural capacity, curated with a source note and refreshed quarterly. Labelled "not warning" and never coloured: a capacity that glows every day is the Christmas-tree failure.
  7. 7Moves & counter-moves. Thirty days on one axis, four dot kinds. Sequence is the predictive signal: a counter-move days after a designation is a pattern; a standing seizure tempo is posture.
  8. 8The read. Per AOR, with a level call, a falsifier and a decision linkage, in the I&W discipline. Generated on open, day-cached, never on page load.
  9. 9Honest gaps. Foreign counter-measures need a feed (EU + UK sanctions CSVs are keyless; PRC MOFCOM notices would be a browser capture). Until then the "United States" rows have no counter-move partner and say so.
