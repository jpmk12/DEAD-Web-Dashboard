Callouts — what changes and why

  1. 1Glance gets a Where you are strip under the clocks: the effective location (TDY › home, the clocks’ own rule), now · high/low · precip · wind, your nearest tracked airfield’s flight category with the TAF turn and the 30-h hazard, and home in one muted phrase. Deterministic, from routes that already exist; a door to the tab.
  2. 2The brief’s Weather & travel lines already exist in the cached brief; they render only in the modal today. They join the open brief card on Glance at zero cost.
  3. 3A sources strip names every feed, what it supplies and whether it is up. Feeds are keyless and fill each other’s gaps (NWS is US-only; Open-Meteo is global; AWC is the aviation truth), so they are not user-removable — what you edit is places and airfields.
  4. 4Cards split into Places (civil forecast points) and My airfields (the aviation read). The same point is never in both.
  5. 5OCONUS cards get a 7-day from Open-Meteo daily (today the trend and next-4 are NWS-only, so the card for where you ARE is the thinnest on the page). Source named on every card.
  6. 6＋ / ✕ on the page: add a place (city, ZIP, coordinates), remove a card, add or remove an airfield — all through the one Track command with Undo. No trip to Preferences.
  7. 7Airfields grouped by combatant command, ordered hub › ★ › spokes › rest, worst field named on the header — the same hierarchy as the OSINT board, so the two tabs read alike. Quiet commands fold to one line.
  8. 8Each field shows its 24-h TAF category bar (the SITREP already computes it), the worst category ahead with its window, the model hazard, a crosswind chip when runways are known, and a SITREP door.
  9. 9UNKNOWN is not VFR — a field with no observation says why, in words, instead of an empty card.
  10. 10Threats by command: alerts, tropical, hazards and disasters under one header per COCOM, worst first, with the source and “near …” on each row. Airfields finally get the 30-h hazard scan (today only home and civil places are scanned).
  11. 11The map follows the selected place or airfield; airfields are pinned on it.
  12. 12Space weather is one sentence in crew terms with four LEDs; the Kp/G/R/S tiles and history fold under it. At a scale of 3+ the sentence says what breaks and when.
