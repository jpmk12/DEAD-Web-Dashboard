Callouts — what changes and why

  1. 1The read never shows “Empty read — please retry”: an empty model reply falls back to the board’s own deterministic summary and retries once when the board lands.
  2. 2Chokepoint interdiction moves up, directly under the read: the strait grades are the evidence most of the actor board rests on. A reopening reads reversal, never a closure act.
  3. 3Every tile says why it is on the board (the AOI or watch that put it there) and can be excluded with ✕.
  4. 4Names that are not countries are skipped and named — Hormuz is a chokepoint, “Tawain” and “Israeli” are misspellings — with one-tap fixes, instead of becoming “UNKNOWN · state” actors that score nonsense.
  5. 5An actor editor: the derived register, exclusions, and additions (a country or a curated non-state actor), stored in the Mission Profile as team config.
  6. 6The driver line says which instrument moved and in which modality, with the learning-mode day count — eight tiles no longer read the same sentence.
  7. 7The timeline gets one lane per actor (“Iran — moves”), so a dot says who moved; pressure lanes are labelled pressure.
  8. 8Hover or tap a dot: date, actor, instrument, grade, headline, source, link. Threats are dotted, acts solid.
  9. 9The coercion board credits a headline to its author, once; the other actors it mentions are listed on the row, not given rows of their own.
  10. 10A reversal grammar (“again flowing”, “reopened”, “resumed transits”, “lifted the blockade”) demotes a closure phrase to the end of an act.
  11. 11A CISA advisory counts as a state act only when it names a state actor or APT; a product advisory about a Chinese vendor is shown and scores nothing.
