# Design review, October 2026 — findings

> Plan: `PLAN-DESIGN-REVIEW.md`. One section per surface, each from a
> walkthrough the operator recorded (screens + a written user story), read
> against the code. Format follows `REVIEW.md`: finding → cause → verdict →
> proposal → size. Verdicts: **Fix** (a defect), **Change** (a design
> choice to revise), **Build** (new capability), **Keep**. A status line at
> the end of each section tracks what shipped.

## 1. Glance (walkthrough 2026-10-05, desktop, 14:27 EDT while TDY in Jordan)

### What the walkthrough says works — Keep

- The clock wall: time, date, daylight phase in one read.
- The brief's Key developments and Suggested focus: relevant to current
  missions and CENTCOM airfields, and it flags closures and the strategic
  pieces worth the time. The F-35/China item was noticed from here.
- "Not looked at since it worsened": it keyed a look. Keep it exactly where
  it is.
- Global Reach Watch: the right airfields and airspace, consistent with the
  other modules.
- Breaking & Critical with the Thesis button: used several times in one
  sitting.

### Findings

**G1 — Zulu belongs at the far left, the rest in UTC order.** *Change, S.*
`renderClocks` sorts every tile west→east by current offset, so New Jersey
(UTC−4) lands left of Zulu. Pin the UTC tile first; sort the remainder by
offset as now. One line in `lib/worldClocks.ts`, one test.

**G2 — The status row has no name.** *Change, S.* The eight tiles sit under
the brief with no heading, so the walkthrough called it "the section below
it". A small label ("RIGHT NOW · live, 5-min") and one line of help make
it legible as the instrument panel it is.

**G3 — Posture tile lands on Russia.** *Change, S–M.* The tile emits
`osint:set-pane regional`, and the Regional rail default-selects its first
worst-severity entry — Russia, RED and chronic for twelve days, the least
newsworthy RED on the board. Land on the *most relevant* location instead:
the one that escalated today, else the TDY country (Jordan today), else the
hub's country, else the worst. The tile already knows all four.

**G4 — Spectrum says Taiwan, click goes to CENTCOM.** *Fix, S.* The tile's
label comes from the cyber indicator's own board, and its click sends
`watch:focus {kind:"iw", id:<that board>}` — but the Watch pane's handler
only opens the I&W panel (`setIwOpen(true)`) and discards the id, so the
panel shows its first board, CENTCOM · Iran. The I&W tile has the same
defect; it merely happened to point at the first board today. Fix:
`WarningBoard` takes a `focusProblemId` and expands and scrolls to it.

**G5 — Demand tile stuck on "loading".** *Fix, M.* Two causes. The status
row awaits all five routes in one `Promise.all`, so the slowest gates every
tile, and a route that fails (a 502 from the gateway on a cold
`getDemandHorizon` fan-out) leaves its tile at "…" until the five-minute
poll. And `/api/demand-horizon` awaits the bare assembly — the one route on
the row that does not follow the bounded-wait / `pending` contract the
spectrum and economy routes use. Fix both: fetch per tile with `allSettled`
and a short retry, and give the demand route `maxWaitMs` + `pending` so a
cold start answers in 8 s with the last body.

**G6 — Alerts before Tasks and Family.** *Change, S.* Reorder the tiles:
Posture · Bases · I&W · Spectrum · Demand · **Alerts** · Tasks · Family.

**G7 — Tasks and Family tooltips say nothing.** *Fix, S.* Both carry a
fixed sentence while every other tile lists its items. `/api/family/week`
already returns lapsed and due-soon titles with days-until; the Glance tab
holds the due tasks. Pass the item lists into the tiles and show them.

**G8 — Needs You Now buries the operator's own items.** *Change, S–M.*
Tasks are pinned in a violet "Your actions" group at the top (right), but
high-priority emails are ranked into the world list (rank 2–3) beneath
force-protection RED rows (rank −2) — the Google security alert sat at the
bottom. The operator's rule is clear: *things for me first, in my colour;
the world after, in its colours.* Move priority emails into "Your actions"
under the tasks, rendered violet; leave force, weather, disasters and OSINT
in the world list in red/amber. The "View all" target logic is unaffected.

**G9 — Which airfields matter most.** *Build, M.* Global Reach Watch ranks
by score, so an ordered departure anywhere outranks a NOTAM at the hub.
The Mission Profile already declares hub and spokes, and a trip declares
where the operator is. Pin rows that touch the hub, a spoke or the TDY
location to the top with a small "mine" mark, then the rest by score. No
new data; one ranking rule, tested.

**G10 — Tomorrow's event shows 3:00 AM; it is at 10:00 IDT.** *Fix, M.*
Not a bug in the arithmetic — 10:00 IDT (UTC+3) is 03:00 EDT, and the
device zone is New York (the clock wall marks it), so Glance and the
brief's schedule both rendered the right instant in the wrong zone for
where the operator is. Three parts: (a) **label the zone** on every time
on Glance's Today/Tomorrow and in the brief's schedule — a bare "3:00 AM"
is the real defect; (b) in Auto mode, **an active trip's zone wins** over
the device zone — the trip has coordinates, Open-Meteo's `timezone=auto`
(already used for the trip's weather) returns the IANA zone, store it on
the trip; Pin still overrides; (c) the tooltip shows the instant in both
zones. *Decision:* confirm that a declared TDY should set the effective
zone in Auto mode.

**G11 — A major posture change was nearly missed.** *Build, M–L.* "B-1s
Empty Out of Fairford" appeared as one newsletter line in On Your Radar.
A deployment, redeployment, surge, bed-down or withdrawal of aircraft is
exactly the demand signal the north star asks for, and nothing in the app
recognises the shape. Proposal: a deterministic **force-posture-move
grammar** (phrases, never words — the `economicWarfare.ts` discipline:
"deploy", "redeploy", "empty out", "stand up", "bed down", "surge",
"forward-deploy", "withdraw" paired with an airframe, unit or base, plus
an AOR resolution) over articles and newsletter bullets → a **Posture
moves** strip on Glance above Breaking, and a recency-decayed, capped
driver on the demand horizon for the AOR named. Falsifier and provenance
like every other sensor. *Decision:* build it in this pass or park it for
the Build phase.

**G12 — The Markets block is six static tickers.** *Change, S–M.* It
informs nothing. Two options: a live mini-ticker (price + day change) using
the Yahoo chart parser already in `lib/energyPrices.ts`, cached 15 min; or
cut it and show Brent plus the chokepoint lead-class strip, which is the
economic signal that actually bears on lift. *Decision:* ticker or cut.

**G13 — The OE brief should open, not download.** *Change, M.* The button
builds the zero-script HTML and forces a download. Open it in a new tab
instead, inside a thin viewer with two buttons — Download HTML and Print /
Save as PDF (the browser's print dialog) — while the exported file stays
script-free. Layout fixes found reading today's export:
- The masthead's AO line is one 300-character run-on; render it as a
  compact list (hub · spokes · theaters · AOIs with their chokepoints).
- "snapshot was stale at export" needs its meaning beside it ("built from a
  cached picture older than 5 min; nothing here is live") and must not sit
  next to a BLUF chip that says "no level change since last look".
- BLUF chips → a four-column line (RED · boards elevated · demand rising ·
  alerts) so it reads at a glance.
- Active alerts grouped by kind (force RED · ordered departure · I&W ·
  cyber) rather than one fourteen-item list.
- I&W boards print the indicator *taxonomy* text; print the current
  **drivers** instead — the taxonomy is the same every day.
- "Mobility commander's declared AO" → the operator's role.
- Team state "No crew state declared — posture against demand cannot be
  judged" is correct and should stay; it is the one line that asks the
  operator to do something.

**G14 — Data hygiene the walkthrough exposed.** *Operator action, S.* The
INDOPACOM AOI is named "Tawain" and the Ukraine AOI's primary list includes
"Stalingrad"; both are Mission Profile entries and both print on every
surface (I&W boards, alerts, the OE brief). Rename in Preferences →
Mission Profile. The app should not auto-correct declared names.

**G15 — The brief said King Hussein "de-escalates to AMBER"; the posture
table says it escalated today.** *Fix, S.* The deterministic record
(`previousComposite` lower than today's AMBER) is the truth; the prose is
the model reading "(was GREEN yesterday)" as a fall. The force line in the
prompt should state direction explicitly — "ESCALATED from GREEN" /
"EASED from RED" — so the model cannot invert it, and the brief's modal
"Today's Schedule" should not be headed *Today* when it holds only
tomorrow's item.

## 2. News — Feed · Threads · History (walkthrough 2026-10-05, desktop, 15:40 EDT)

Mockups first, as asked: `docs/news-threads.png` (the proposed landing view)
and `docs/news-read.png` (the proposed Read view), from
`docs/mockups/news-threads.html` / `news-read.html`. Numbered callouts on
each render match the proposals below.

### What the walkthrough says works — Keep

- Newsletters: real value — they carry current events and point at sources
  the operator trusts (Politico, A&SF). The problem is volume, not content.
- Threads: "the thing I find the most value in" — the balance of strategic
  depth and current events, with the AMC sentence at the end of each
  summary. Keep the model, the through-line, the per-thread summaries and
  the newsletter quote box exactly as they are.
- Glance → Breaking & critical remains the busy-day must-read path; nothing
  here should weaken it.

### What the operator is reading for

Two things, stated plainly in the story, and the tab should be organised
around them rather than around source categories:

1. **Strategic depth or insight** — long-form analysis (War on the Rocks,
   Foreign Affairs, the War Zone features).
2. **Current events that touch an AMC operational leader** — posture,
   chokepoints, pay/OPTEMPO tells, allied deliveries, HADR.

Everything else is "nice to have". Today the Feed shows all three mixed in
one grid behind eight category tabs, and the second thing the operator
wants (Threads) is a second page.

### Findings

**N1 — Threads is on the second page; it should be the first.** *Change, M.*
The News tab lands on Feed (newsletters, then the trending strip, then the
category grid). The view the operator values most is a click away and
greyed until the feed has loaded. Make Threads the landing view. Cost rule
(the AI-spend discipline): `/api/threads` is the most expensive call in the
app (Opus over ~40 articles, about $0.42) and is already day-cached by
article hash, so a Threads-first landing costs one call per distinct article
set per day. Add the brief's generation cap (3 per day unless ↻ Regenerate)
so a feed that moves through the day cannot spend more than that
unattended; past the cap, serve the last session and say so.

**N2 — Three views where two will do: fold History into Threads.** *Change,
M.* History holds the best data on the tab (label trajectories, sustained
escalation, re-emergence, the search over every past thread) and nobody
reaches it. The walkthrough asked both for "trending sorted by rising and
falling" and for History "to better inform me"; both are the same
proposal. Signals becomes a **Moving** rail beside the threads, sorted
sustained-escalation → rising → re-emerging → steady → fading, each row with
its run ("3rd day") and a 14-day sparkline. Labels becomes the sparkline +
run on every thread card (N4) and the drawer that opens from a Moving row.
Sessions becomes the date picker in the header ("▾ Oct 5") and two "Past
days" rows at the foot of the rail. The search box stays, in the drawer.
Two views remain: **Threads** and **Read**.

**N3 — The through-line buries the AMC sentence; no diff against
yesterday.** *Change, S.* The model already ends the through-line with
"For AMC: …"; render that sentence in the accent colour so it reads first.
Add one line under it — "vs yesterday: Bab el-Mandeb is NEW; Iran war
stable → rising; Ukraine unchanged" — computed by a pure diff of today's
session against the previous one (same `thread_sessions` rows History
reads). No model call.

**N4 — Thread cards carry no history.** *Build, M.* Each card shows an
arrow the model chose today and nothing else. Join the card to its label's
history: the sparkline, the run ("↑ Rising · 3rd day", "new today",
"fading"), and a **Δ yesterday** line (trend change, sources added or
dropped, headline shift) from the previous session's thread with the same
label. Pure, from stored sessions; `getLabelHistory` already exists.

**N5 — "So what · AMC" is a grey sentence at the end of a paragraph.**
*Change, S.* Split the model's final AMC sentence out of the summary into
its own accented line on the card (prompt: a separate `amc` field, one
sentence; fallback: the last sentence when it names AMC/mobility/lift).

**N6 — A thread cannot be acted on.** *Build, M–L.* The data the operator
wants to exploit has no doors out of the card. Four, all on things the app
already has: **Ask about this** (seeds the assistant with the thread —
N10); **Save to Docs** (creates or appends a 🧵 `thread` doc whose "Trace"
section the Docs tab already renders as a timeline — one entry per day:
date, trend, headline, sources; re-saving appends, never duplicates);
**Follow** (adds the label to the watchlist, which already pins matching
threads ⚑ and feeds Glance/alerts); and a door-in chip to the surface the
thread touches — the I&W board whose AOI it names, the chokepoint read, the
demand-horizon row (deterministic name match on label + AOI countries).

**N7 — Trending is unsorted and unlabelled.** *Change, S.* `TrendStrip`
renders the ten non-steady movers in API order with a glyph each. Group and
order them: ▲ rising (by velocity), ✦ new, ▼ fading; show count and ×
velocity; make a chip filter the lanes below (dispatch `docs:search`-style
into the feed filter). `classifyMovers` already computes state and
velocity.

**N8 — The newsletter stack.** *Change, M.* Fourteen unread rows, oldest
five days, with "13 series you've never opened" above them; the digest
button sits at the right of the heading. Reframe the section as a **queue**:
per-source unread counts and the age of the oldest; **Catch me up** (the
existing Digest) as the primary action; **Mark all read** for the days
there is no time; and by default only the rows that *earned* one — a
watchlist hit (⚑ houthis) or a thread match (thread · Iran war), with the
reason on the row — the routine rest behind "show all". Nothing is hidden
from the digest. The "never opened" line moves to the footer as "review
sources".

**N9 — One grid, eight tabs; the two things the operator reads for are not
lanes.** *Change, M.* Replace the primary category tabs with two lanes:
**Strategic depth** — long-form from the analysis sources (WOTR, Foreign
Affairs, CSIS, RAND, Lawfare, War Zone features), decided by source + body
length, deterministic, with read time — and **Operational now** — today's
AI-curated "Critical for you" with the AMC reason on the card (the curation
prompt already judges relevance; ask it for the reason in six words). Each
card names the thread it belongs to (article ids are on the thread). The
category tabs survive as filter chips under Trending. **Everything else
folds** under one heading with its count — the existing "More to discover"
affordance, generalised.

**N10 — Two assistants on one tab.** *Change, M.* The right-hand "News
analyst" (`NewsChatPanel` → `/api/news-chat`) and the floating assistant
(`FloatingAssistant` → `/api/chat`) are both Opus with a system prompt and
a context block; they differ only in what they are handed. The analyst has
40 articles, the newsletters and the threads, a reader persona, and a
"remember more/less of" instruction; no calendar, no OE picture, no actions,
no memory, and the conversation dies when the tab changes. The assistant
has calendar, tasks, 20 articles, 10 newsletters, the OE snapshot, memory,
docs, the action grammar, and a thread that survives close, reload and
tab changes — but not the threads analysis. **Yes, they do the same thing,
and they should be one.** Merge: the client passes `threads` (and the full
article set the News tab holds) to `/api/chat` when the surface is news;
the chat's system block gains the analyst's two persona lines and the
more/less-of preference instruction; the News tab's rail becomes a docked
view of the same `ChatPanel` (same thread), and the thread cards' "Ask about
this" seeds it. Retire `NewsChatPanel` and `/api/news-chat` (one fewer Opus
route, one fewer ledger label). Token cost is a wash: the assistant already
sends articles; threads add ~1.5k input tokens on this surface only, inside
the cacheable block.

**N11 — Threads do not reach Glance or the brief.** *Build, S–M.* Glance's
Breaking & critical is the curated article set; the brief prompt gets
trend movers but not thread trajectories. Two cheap joins once N2/N4 exist:
a "rising threads" line in the brief prompt (label, run, headline — no new
model call, the brief is already paid for), and the ⚑ followed threads as
rows on Glance's Breaking panel when they are rising. Deliberately not a
Threads tile on the status row: a thread is reading, not status.

### Decisions needed

1. **Threads as the News landing view** (N1) — recommended yes, with the
   3-per-day generation cap.
2. **Fold History into Threads** (N2) — recommended yes; History's three
   sub-tabs become the Moving rail, the card trajectories and the date
   picker. Nothing is deleted from the store.
3. **Merge the News analyst into the assistant** (N10) — recommended yes.
4. **Newsletter default = earned rows only** (N8) — recommended yes;
   "show all" is one click and the digest sees everything.

## 3. Calendar (walkthrough 2026-10-05, desktop, 16:00 EDT)

Mockup first, as with News: `docs/calendar.png` from
`docs/mockups/calendar-proposed.html`; callout numbers match the proposals.

### What the walkthrough says works — Keep

- The family dates reaching the calendar at all (they used to live on four
  lists that never met the week view). The *information* is right; the
  *placement* is not.
- Keep in Touch and Tasks: both wanted, both stay on the tab.
- The per-event quick actions (AI-edit / edit / nudge / delete) and the
  per-email ➕ / 📅 convert buttons on the Email tab — the machinery for
  "email → calendar" exists; it is just one card at a time and lives on the
  other tab.

### Findings

**C1 — Late family items are the first thing on the agenda, under past
days.** *Fix, S–M.* The agenda unions event days with family-date days, and
`familyDates` keeps a 7-day lookback for late items, so the screen opens on
"Thursday October 1" and "Friday October 2" — past days with LATE rows —
before Tomorrow. A calendar that starts in the past reads as broken. Past
and late items belong in a **Don't miss** list at the top (C3); the agenda
starts today.

**C2 — The same obligation three times.** *Fix, S.* "Scholastic Book Fair —
Set Up E-Wallet", "— set up e-wallet for Abigail", "e-wallet setup": one
deadline extracted from three newsletters. `deadlineKey` is (message +
normalised title) by design — title alone duplicated on rewording, message
alone merged distinct obligations — so three messages mentioning one thing
yield three stored rows. Dedupe at RENDER time, never in the store: same
date + token overlap ≥ 0.6 on the title → one row carrying "×3 newsletters"
and all three source links (`dedupeFamilyDates`, pure, tested). The same
join applies to "Picture Day" ×3 on Oct 15.

**C3 — Nothing says "you are about to miss this".** *Build, M.* Four
sources each keep their own overdue state — family deadlines (LATE), tasks
(Overdue), keep-in-touch (overdue / never contacted), and tasks due this
week — in four places on one screen. One **Don't miss** panel at the top:
late and due-today across all four, deduplicated, each row with its source
chip and the action it needs (Done / Snooze / Open email for family; ✓ /
⏭ tmrw for tasks; ✓ Contacted / 📅 Schedule for people). Pure join over
data already on the page. This is the walkthrough's sentence: "help me
better see my important events and not miss anything".

**C4 — The rail collapse no longer makes sense.** *Change, S.* "◁ collapse"
shrinks Keep in Touch + Tasks to a vertical "Tasks" strip. It dates from
when the rail held the assistant; the operator wants both panels. Remove
the collapse; the rail is always there on desktop and stacks below the
agenda on a phone. Fold INSIDE the panels instead: Tasks groups fold (Later
and No date closed by default), the add-form date field hides behind
"+ date", Keep in Touch keeps its "caught up" fold.

**C5 — Important dates in email have no road to the calendar except one
card at a time.** *Build, M–L.* Today the only paths are the per-email 📅
on the Email tab (Claude plans an event, you review, it creates), the
Family digest's deadlines, and Quick capture. Nothing *proactive*: the email
triage already reads every unread email with a model and writes a summary,
but extracts no date. Add one field to that SAME call (the Family
proposals trick — no second call): `dates: [{ when: YYYY-MM-DD | null,
whenText, what }]` for emails that state a specific date or deadline, with
the rule the Family tab already enforces — **the model must not resolve
"next Friday"**; an unanchored phrase comes back `when: null` and renders
as "open email", never as a date. A **Dates in your mail** panel on the
Calendar lists them (7-day window, dismissals per user in the UI-state
store), each with **＋ Event** (the existing `/api/gmail/convert` create
path with the date pre-filled, 30-minute default, review inline) / **＋
Task** / **✕**. Owner and crew alike (it is the user's own mail). Cost:
zero new model calls; a few more output tokens per triage.

**C6 — Times in the wrong zone, unlabelled.** *Fix, S.* "Better Up 3:00 AM
– 3:45 AM" is the Glance G10 bug on this tab: `formatEventTime` uses the
device zone. Apply `useEffectiveZone` + `timeInZone` + the zone label (and
the device time in the tooltip), exactly as Glance now does; the Today
strip (C7) names the zone once.

**C7 — No "today" anchor.** *Change, S.* The header is "Upcoming" with the
account email. A **Today strip** — the date in the effective zone, today's
events inline, and the three counts (overdue tasks · family due · check-ins
due) — gives the page its anchor and the rail its summary.

**C8 — Family rows look like events.** *Change, S.* Interleaved at the same
weight as events with a violet left border, they read as peers. Render them
as a quieter dashed register UNDER the day's events, with the kind glyph
named (⚑ deadline · $ bill · ⬒ document) and a "Family →" door, not a
whole-row button.

**C9 — Trips never reach the calendar.** *Build, S.* The app knows the
active TDY (it drives the brief, the zone and the local news) but the
agenda never shows it. A "TDY · Amman — day 5 of 9" chip on each day header
inside a trip, from `/api/trips`.

### Decisions needed

1. **Date extraction rides the email triage call** (C5) — recommended yes;
   it is the same discipline as Family proposals (same call, one more
   field, never a guessed date).
2. **Past-due family items leave the agenda entirely** and live only in
   Don't miss (C1/C3) — recommended yes; the agenda starts today.
3. **Remove the rail collapse** (C4) — recommended yes.

### Ranked

| # | Item | Verdict | Size | Needs a decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | C3 Don't miss (one list across family / tasks / people) | Build | M | yes (2) |
| 2 | C1 + C2 agenda starts today; family rows deduplicated | Fix | S–M | yes (2) |
| 3 | C5 Dates in your mail (triage field + panel + ＋ Event / ＋ Task) | Build | M–L | yes (1) |
| 4 | C4 remove the rail collapse; fold inside the panels | Change | S | yes (3) |
| 5 | C6 effective zone + labels on event times | Fix | S | no |
| 6 | C7 Today strip | Change | S | no |
| 7 | C8 family rows as a quieter register with kind glyphs | Change | S | no |
| 8 | C9 TDY chip on day headers | Build | S | no |

**Status (2026-10-05, later):** all three decisions taken as recommended and
ALL eight items built (C1–C9). The date extraction rides the triage call

## 4. Email (walkthrough 2026-10-06, desktop, 16:18 EDT)

Mockup first, as with News and Calendar: `docs/email.png` from
`docs/mockups/email-proposed.html`; callout numbers match the proposals.

### What the walkthrough says works — Keep

- Action items at the top, read first, and "+ Task" from a row: this is how
  an important email became a task in one tap. Keep the panel where it is.
- The three-tier triage itself. The operator trusts it enough to use the
  Low pass as the main workflow — scan Low, confirm nothing important,
  clear it. The complaint is not the tiers, it is that the tiers cannot be
  argued with.
- Mark as read in bulk. "I primarily use this tab to sift through all the
  unimportant emails and mark them read so they don't clutter my inbox."
  That sentence is the tab's job; everything below serves it.

### What the operator is doing

The sift: Action items → Low (verify every row, catch the one the model
got wrong, clear the rest) → Medium (glance) → High (read). Four asks
come straight out of it: promote and demote an email; see WHY an email got
its priority so the system can learn; a Keep that no bulk action can mark
read; and a layout elegant enough that the Low pass is a scan, not a
reading exercise.

### Findings

**E1 — There is no way to promote or demote an email.** *Build, M.* The
walkthrough's exact moment: a Low email that should be High, and "there is
no easy way to promote it" — so the operator selects the other nine by hand
and clears them around it. The code has no per-email override at all:
priority is the Haiku call's answer, then `vipSenders` forces High and
`muteSenders` forces Low (`app/api/gmail/route.ts:186-200`), and those two
lists are editable only in Preferences → Email rules. Make the priority
badge a menu: **High / Medium / Low for this email** (it moves to that group
now) and **Always High / Always Low from this sender** (the existing
`/api/user-prefs/append` door — one tap, no Preferences trip, and
`muteSenders` finally gets a caller). Every change is stored per message in
a new `email_prefs` table (user, account, message id, `priority_set`, the
model's `priority_model`, sender, subject, `keep`, timestamp): the override
survives refresh and devices, and it is the raw material for E3. The
override applies AFTER the cache lookup and the VIP/mute rules, so a
deliberate per-email call always wins.

**E2 — Keep: an email no bulk action may mark read.** *Build, S–M.* The
ask verbatim: "a keep button so emails never get marked read if I have
them marked as keep". Today the only protection is not selecting it. A
`keep` flag on the same `email_prefs` row, a ⚑ Kept chip on the card and a
Kept filter chip. **Both ends enforce it**: the client drops kept ids from
"Mark all read", the group buttons and select-all (the header says "2 kept
stay"), AND `/api/gmail/mark-read` refuses kept ids for that user — the
same server-boundary rule as `/api/family/event`, because a shortcut that
bypasses the client must not clear a kept email. Keep is the app's flag,
not a Gmail star (decision 1). Kept emails are listed first in their group
and never age out of the view while unread.

**E3 — Priority is a black box, and corrections teach nothing.** *Build,
M–L.* "I would like to better understand how priority is determined so the
system can learn." Two halves, both earned by the same correction stream:
- **Why this priority.** The classifier returns a summary but no reason.
  Add ONE field to the SAME Haiku call — `why`, a ≤12-word clause naming
  what fired ("addressed to you · decision requested · priority topic
  CENTCOM airlift"; "mass mailing · no action") — cached beside the
  classification (`email_classification_cache.why`, additive). The
  deterministic rules name themselves ("Always High — VIP sender", "Always
  Low — muted domain"), and an override reads "you set High (model said
  Low)". No second call, a few output tokens per email.
- **Learning from corrections** — `lib/emailLearning.ts`, PURE, tested,
  no model call: (a) **Suggested sender rules**: the same sender corrected
  the same direction ≥ 3 times in 30 d → "You promoted 3 from AFCYP — make
  the sender Always High?" with Yes / Not this sender (dismissal
  permanent, in the existing `dismissed_vip_suggestions` namespace —
  every row states its evidence, same rule as the watchlist suggestions).
  Mark-read-without-opening counts as a weak demotion for the Low rule
  only when the model said Medium or High. (b) **Corrections as examples
  for the next triage**: the 15 most recent corrections ("from · subject →
  High; model said Low") ride into the classifier as a few-shot block
  OUTSIDE the prompt hash, so a correction never re-classifies the cached
  inbox — it shapes only emails not yet seen. (c) **How priority is
  decided**, a strip above the inbox: role, counts of priority topics /
  VIP / muted, corrections in 30 d, a "what the model reads" fold showing
  the actual rules text, and "Rules ⚙" → `?prefs=email`. The Suggested
  VIPs panel (reply-count mining) folds into this strip as a third kind
  of suggested rule instead of sitting above Action items.

**E4 — The Low pass is the main workflow and the layout fights it.**
*Change, M.* Filter chips carry no counts; every Low email renders as a
full card (sender, account pill, badge, five icons, subject, two-line
summary) when the operator is scanning subjects; the only per-email read
is select → bulk bar; clicking a card toggles selection, which surprises
on a phone. Proposed: the list is **always grouped High → Medium → Low**
with a count and its own **"✓ Mark N read"** on each group header (kept
excluded and said so); chips show counts and jump/filter; **Low rows are
compact** (checkbox · sender · subject · why · age · badge · ✓) and open to
the full card on tap; High and Medium stay full cards; a **✓ Read** on
every row; the checkbox selects, the row opens; the account pill becomes a
violet dot shown only when a second account is connected. Keyboard triage
for the sift: j/k move · x select · e read · h keep · 1/2/3 priority.

**E5 — A failed mark-read looks like success.** *Fix, S.* `markAsRead`
only logs failures and the route answers `{ok:true}` regardless;
`EmailTab.markEmailsRead` never checks `res.ok`; and the mark-read route
decrypts the secondary token without refreshing it (`decryptToken`, not
`getValidSecondaryToken`), so an expired secondary token passes the check
and every modify fails. The email vanishes from the list and the cache,
stays unread in Gmail, and returns on the next refresh — which teaches the
operator the button is broken. Route uses the refreshing accessor,
`markAsRead` returns the failed ids, the route returns them, the client
puts them back with a toast.

**E6 — Action items forget, and re-pay.** *Fix, S–M.* `actionsChecked`
and the task/doc status live only in component state — a refresh or
another device shows every row unchecked, including ones already in
Tasks. And the panel POSTs the whole High+Medium set to Opus whenever the
id set is not a subset of the last run, so one new arrival re-extracts
twenty emails. Persist checked ids + "added to Tasks/Doc" per action
(key = message id + action text hash) in the UI-state store (cross-
device); cache extracted actions per MESSAGE id server-side (an email's
action does not change) so only unseen emails go to the model.

**E7 — Priority chips colour All and Low the same emerald; the badge count
is High-only.** *Change, XS.* Folded into E4's chip row: All neutral, High
red, Medium amber, Low slate, Kept violet, each with its count. The nav
badge stays High-only (that is the right alarm).

### Decisions needed

1. **Keep is the app's own flag, not a Gmail star** (E2) — recommended
   yes. A star is a different promise ("important"), and starring from
   here would make Gmail's Starred view a side effect of a triage action.
   Can be added later as an option if wanted.
2. **Corrections ride into the classifier as examples, outside the cache
   hash** (E3b) — recommended yes. The alternative (put them in the hash)
   re-classifies the whole cached inbox on every correction: ~50 Haiku
   classifications per tap.
3. **Always-grouped list with compact Low rows replaces the flat filtered
   list** (E4) — recommended yes; the chips remain as counts and jumps so
   nothing is lost.

### Ranked

| # | Item | Verdict | Size | Needs a decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | E1 promote / demote + Always-from-sender (badge menu, `email_prefs`) | Build | M | no |
| 2 | E2 Keep (flag, chip, both-ends enforcement) | Build | S–M | yes (1) |
| 3 | E3 why line + suggested rules + corrections-as-examples + the strip | Build | M–L | yes (2) |
| 4 | E4 grouped list, compact Low rows, per-row ✓, keyboard triage | Change | M | yes (3) |
| 5 | E5 mark-read reports failures; secondary token refreshed | Fix | S | no |
| 6 | E6 action items persisted + cached per message | Fix | S–M | no |
| 7 | E7 chip colours and counts | Change | XS | no |

**Status (2026-10-06, later):** all three decisions taken as recommended
and ALL seven items built (E1–E7). Keep is the app's own flag enforced in
the mark-read route; corrections ride into the classifier as a second
system block outside the cache hash; the list is always grouped with
compact Low rows. One E3 detail NOT built: mark-read-without-opening as a
weak demotion signal (it needs an open-tracking event the tab does not
record; suggested rules come from explicit corrections only). See
CLAUDE.md "Email tab rebuilt".

## 5. Family — School · Household (walkthrough 2026-10-06, desktop, 16:47 EDT)

Mockup first: `docs/family-proposed.png` from
`docs/mockups/family-proposed.html`; callout numbers match the proposals.

### What the walkthrough says works — Keep

- The per-person summaries: "I really like the summaries for the kids."
  The ask is that they keep up, not that they change shape.
- "While you are away": the operator reads it as the list of what they are
  missing, to talk to the kids about on the call. Keep it, and make it
  that list.
- The proposals note ("your mail already mentions these") earned a look:
  the operator explored it to see whether it mattered. Keep the mechanism;
  fix what it shows.
- Household's division of labour (model extracts facts, cadence and
  runway computed) was not questioned. Keep.

### What the operator is doing

Open the tab, check the note about something tracked, read While you are
away for the call home, scan Needs you — and hit three things: items
already marked done still listed, items marked "No date" whose text names
the date, and summaries that do not visibly move as new mail arrives.

### Findings

**F1 — Done items stay in Needs you.** *Fix, S.* `tracked` is every
stored row with no state filter (`listDeadlines`), the header count
includes done and dismissed, and the rows render dimmed with strikethrough
and an "undo" button (`FamilyTab.tsx:393-452`). Five of the seven rows in
the walkthrough were done. After a Done tap nothing reloads, so the rollup
line and the per-person counts stay stale. Done and Not-mine rows LEAVE the
list into one folded line at the bottom ("5 done this fortnight — show ·
undo"); the count is open rows only; state changes re-pull the rollup.

**F2 — The same obligation three times.** *Fix, S.* Scholastic e-wallet
×3, Class T-shirt ×2 — `deadlineKey` is (message + title) by design, so one
deadline in three newsletters is three rows. The Calendar already dedupes
at render (`dedupeFamilyDates`); apply the same rule here: same person,
same date (or both undated), title-token overlap ≥ 0.6 → one row carrying
"×3 newsletters"; Done / Not mine / Set date apply to every merged id. The
store is untouched.

**F3 — "No date" when the email names the date.** *Build, M.* "Picture
Day is October 15" sits in the detail of a row chipped NO DATE. The model
was right not to invent an ORDER deadline, but the operator cannot assign
one: no route accepts a due date (`PATCH /api/family` takes only `state`
and `snoozeUntil`), and the only way a date ever arrives is a later
extraction filling a null (`COALESCE(due_iso)`). The ask verbatim: "I would
like to be able to assign dates under the later." Build: (a) `PATCH`
accepts `dueIso` (yyyy-mm-dd or null) → `setDeadlineDue`, written with
`due_source = 'user'` so an extraction never overwrites a date the operator
set; (b) **Later becomes one menu**: the dates the email itself names,
offered as one-tap chips with the sentence they came from
(`datesInText(detail, today)`, PURE — explicit month-and-day or m/d forms
only, never a relative phrase), a date picker, and the existing snoozes.
Nothing is assigned automatically — the chip is the operator's tap, which
is exactly the discipline "never a guessed date" permits.

**F4 — The kids' summaries do not keep up.** *Build, M–L.* The summary is
REWRITTEN from the last 14 days of mail on each run, from a 15-minute
in-process cache keyed on the roster; the tab unmounts on every tab switch
and only re-reads on mount; nothing marks what is new; an obligation
older than 14 days falls out of the prose even though its row is still
tracked; and a failed model call renders an empty card with no message.
"I want them to be updated as new things surface in my email." Build a
**running brief per person** (`family_person_brief`: person, text,
source ids, updated_at): on each digest the model gets the PREVIOUS brief
plus only the messages it has not seen, and is asked to UPDATE it — keep
what still stands, drop what has passed, add what is new, ≤120 words.
Unchanged mail → no call. The card header says "updated 2 h ago · 2 new";
lines first seen after the operator's last visit (`surface_state`
"family" last-look, the OE delta's own mechanism) carry a `new` chip, and
the new sentences in the prose are marked. While the tab is open and
visible it checks for new mail every 10 minutes — a Gmail list of ids, no
model call — and re-reads only when new ids appear (decision 2). The
digest's 15-minute cache stays for everything else.

**F5 — Empty cards and silent failure.** *Fix, XS.* Denise renders a
header with nothing under it; a failed call shows blank summaries. A
person with no mail this fortnight says so and names the last noted item;
a failed call says "summary unavailable — the deadlines below are from the
record".

**F6 — While you are away repeats the trip on every row and includes past
trips.** *Change, S–M.* Every row reads "falls on …, while you are in
Jordan (2026-08-25 → 2027-03-01)"; `listTrips` returns every trip, past
and future, so a lapsed deadline inside a trip that ended months ago still
matches. Name the trip ONCE in the header (label, dates, day N of M);
split **Happened since you left** (dated items inside the trip whose date
has passed — including done ones, for 14 days) from **Before you are
back**; group by person; only the current or next trip matches; a "copy as
a list" button for the call. This is the list the operator asked for.

**F7 — Three lists show the same five dates.** *Change, S.* The per-person
"upcoming" lines, the "Dates found" card (+ Add) and While you are away all
list the same events. Put "＋ Add" on the per-person lines (and "on
calendar ✓" once added) and retire the separate Dates found card; the
unanchored ones ("open the email to set a date") become one quiet line
under the person.

**F8 — The proposal shows a raw link.** *Fix, S.* The SignUpGenius row's
evidence is the URL the mail contained. Show the subject of the email it
came from, the sentence around the mention, who it concerns when a person
is implied, what tracking will do ("its own emails are read for Abigail
from the next digest"), and "open the email" as a small affordance. Same
for Household's "no address seen" row.

**F9 — Orientation.** *Change, S.* Proposals and discovery sit ABOVE the
content on both panes, and the subtitle says nothing live. An attention
line under the header — needs you · undated-but-named · lands while away ·
to file · updated N min ago · new since your last visit — and the
proposals + folded discovery move BELOW the kids. Household: the discovery
card proposed AFCYP (a school/activity sender) as a biller; route
discovery rows by category to the pane that owns them.

### Decisions needed

1. **Done rows leave the list** into a folded line with undo (F1), rather
   than staying dimmed in place — recommended yes.
2. **Running brief per person, with a 10-minute new-mail check while the
   tab is open** (F4) — recommended yes. The check is a Gmail list only;
   the model runs only when new message ids exist, so the cost is the same
   as today's "refresh on open" and the brief is never rewritten from
   scratch.
3. **Dates named in the email are offered as chips, never assigned** (F3)
   — recommended yes.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | F1 done rows fold away; counts reload | Fix | S | yes (1) |
| 2 | F3 Set date: PATCH dueIso + named-date chips + picker in the Later menu | Build | M | yes (3) |
| 3 | F4 running brief per person, new markers, 10-min new-mail check | Build | M–L | yes (2) |

## 6. OSINT — the command board (walkthrough 2026-10-06, desktop, 17:46 EDT)

A redesign, not a fix list, so this section is a design document with the
findings folded in. **Full mockup first, no code changed**:
`docs/osint-commands.png` (the landing) and `docs/osint-drill.png` (one
command drilled to a country and an airfield), from
`docs/mockups/osint-commands.html` / `osint-drill.html` (shared
`osint.css`); callout numbers run 1–15 across both pages.

### What the walkthrough says works — Keep

- The map, whole — layers, presets, the force-posture rail, Demand read.
- The list under the map ("Significant disasters", which already lists
  kinetic events, NEO, severe weather and tropical systems too) with its
  reach legs to the nearest CRF.
- Everything the panes compute: the I&W boards, the SITREP assembly, the
  Regional dossier, the demand horizon, the OE delta. None of it is wrong;
  none of it is ARRANGED.

### What the operator is asking for

"A logical workflow to consume this information": start at the combatant
commands, see each command's indicator at a glance, drill into a command
→ the region and the countries that matter → the airfields being tracked
and their SITREPs; declare which commands, countries and airfields are
must-tracks; and a prepared primer that says where to drill first.

### Why the tab reads as confusing — what the code says

**O1 — Nothing is organised by command; four things group by command on
their own.** `ForceWatchBoard` groups posture by `cocom`; the events list
under the map groups by `aor`; the Regional rail groups leftovers by
COCOM after the AOIs; the demand horizon is one row per AOR. The I&W
boards carry the command only inside the label string
(`"${seed.aor} · ${seed.name}"`) — `ProblemGeo` has no `aor` field, and
the demand horizon recomputes a board's AOR from its bbox centre, which
can disagree with the AOI's declared `aor`. Each surface answers "what is
going on in CENTCOM" differently and none of them answers it together.

**O2 — The Watch pane is a stack, not a hierarchy.** I&W cards → SITREP
tiles → a "Forming" fold → the map (with the posture rail INSIDE it) →
the events list. Two of those are the same thing at different levels (a
board is about a command; a tile is about an airfield in a country in a
command) and the stack never says so. Clicking ANY I&W card opens ONE
shared board below the whole strip; opening a SITREP tile renders a
second copy of the LED strip inside `SitrepPanel`.

**O3 — Regional is the country level of the same hierarchy, on another
pane.** `GroundTruthTab`'s dossier (posture, conflict reporting, incidents
and mini-map, news, civil, health, spectrum) is exactly what belongs under
a country row; its rail groups AOIs first, then own force, then COCOM —
a second, parallel hierarchy.

**O4 — No notion of a must-track exists.** `MissionProfile` has `theaters`
(unordered, used only for the summary line, the "My AO" preset and feed
suggestions), AOIs with `intensity: primary | watch`, and own-force
hub/spokes. Nothing ranks commands, countries or airfields; the
Regional rail is in declaration order; SITREP bases are the first four
candidates the user confirmed on the Apply screen (`MAX_BASES = 4`).

**O5 — Nothing says where to look first.** The OE delta knows what
changed since the last look; the demand horizon knows which command is
rising; alerts know what is red; the Glance "Needs you now" ranks six
world rows; the convergence and reactivation cards sit in a closed fold.
No surface composes these into "drill here first" with a door to the
place.

**O6 — Smaller faults the survey turned up**: the board's `hubs` are the
first 8 entries of `ALL_AIRFIELDS` inside the bbox, not the operator's
own fields; `SitrepSummary.worse` compares wx/ops/threat only (infra and
spectrum never flag "worse than yesterday"); the map's AOR chips show
only commands that currently have an event, so a quiet must-track command
has no chip; the Feeds pane fetches aircraft/ships only while open while
the map fetches its own; `watch:focus` lands on a shared board rather
than the card.

### The design

One scrolling page under the **Commands** chip (Watch and Regional retire
into it; **Feeds** and **Sources** remain as secondary chips):

1. **Where to look first** (callout 2) — a ranked primer, DETERMINISTIC
   and free: over the OE snapshot the app already keeps (posture rows with
   escalation and chronicity, boards with level/anomaly/trajectory,
   SITREP LEDs with worse-than-yesterday, demand outlooks, alerts, the
   per-user delta, convergence, reactivation, decisions due). Ranking:
   escalated-today red › board at alert › worse-than-yesterday ★ airfield
   › board at warning deteriorating › demand rising against thin crews ›
   forming convergence › calls due; a ★ must-track outranks a non-★ at
   the same tier. Every line carries a door (`→ CENTCOM › Jordan › OJAQ`)
   that opens exactly that level. A "✦ Read (AI)" spends a model call ONLY
   on tap (the same rule as Mobility read); the footer names quiet
   commands as "absence of signal, not evidence of calm" and any source
   that is down.
2. **My airfields** (3) — hub, spokes and ★ fields with their five LEDs
   and driver, pinned above the board: the SITREP for a field you care
   about is one click from anywhere, regardless of which command it sits
   in. This is the answer to "not sure of the best way to track
   SITREPs": the hierarchy carries airfields under their country, AND the
   ones you declared are pinned at the top.
3. **The command board** (4) — one row per combatant command: I&W (worst
   board level, anomaly, trajectory, board count) · posture (dots, red /
   amber counts, escalations) · bases (LEDs, worse-than-yesterday) ·
   demand 7 d (direction, score, confidence) · events 24 h (kinetic, NEO,
   new) · Δ since your last look (worse / better). ★ rows first, then
   worst; a one-line WHY per row; commands you do not track fold into one
   quiet line (6) — present, never hidden.
4. **Drill** (9–14) — a command row expands in place to: its I&W boards
   as compact cards (10; full board on tap) → its countries, ★ first then
   worst, each with posture + direction + chronicity, top driver, the
   pinned field's LED, Δ (11) → a country expands to its **situation
   room** = the Regional dossier rendered inside the row (12) → its
   airfields: ★ fields with five LEDs that expand to the full SITREP
   inline (13, 14); non-★ fields show posture only with "★ to get a
   SITREP". A breadcrumb replaces the subtitle while drilled in; the
   primer and My airfields fold to one line. Every level collapses on a
   second click; the other commands stay as one-line rows.
5. **Must-tracks** (5) — a ★ on any command, country or airfield row,
   stored in the Mission Profile as `mustTrack: { aors[], countries[],
   icaos[] }` (team config, owner edits, same as the rest of the
   profile). ★ drives: sort order and never-fold; the primer's tie-break;
   which airfields get a full SITREP (`MAX_BASES` 4 → 6, ★ fields first,
   hub always); the map's "My AO" preset (= ★ commands); and the AOR chip
   row (every ★ command always has a chip, quiet or not).
6. **The map** (7, 15) — retained whole. Its AOR filter FOLLOWS THE BOARD:
   opening a command flies the map there and narrows the force rail and
   the events list to it; opening a country or field flies to it. The chip
   row still allows a manual override ("All" or any command).
7. **Significant events** (8) — renamed from "Significant disasters"
   (it never was only disasters), grouped by command, following the
   board, with a "near my airfields" lens and new-since-your-last-look
   markers; the "All disasters" expander stays underneath.

### What this needs in the model (the build, in order)

- **A per-command rollup** (`lib/commandBoard.ts`, PURE, tested): one
  `CommandRow` per AOR from the OE snapshot + demand outlooks + events +
  the per-user delta, with `why`. The I&W board gains an `aor` field
  (`ProblemGeo.aor` from the seed; CENTCOM_IRAN → CENTCOM) so the board
  and the demand horizon stop deriving it from the bbox. `/api/commands`
  (bounded, `pending` contract) returns rows + the primer.
- **The primer** (`lib/primer.ts`, PURE, tested): the ranking above.
- **Must-tracks** in the Mission Profile (`sanitizeMustTrack`; editor
  stars on the board + the existing Mission Profile editor); `deriveTracking`
  orders SITREP candidates ★-first and the cap moves to 6.
- **The country row** = `ForceAssessment` rows joined to the dossier on
  expand (`/api/ground-truth?country=` as today, lazily); the airfield row
  = `SitrepSummary` joined to the SITREP on expand (`/api/sitrep?icao=`,
  lazily — nothing assembles until opened, the SITREP cache stays 10 min).
- `CrisisMap` takes `aorFilter` and `flyTo` as props from the board (it
  already handles `crisis-map:flyto`); the events list takes the same
  filter; `ForceWatchBoard` loses its own COCOM select.
- Retire: `WatchPane`'s stack, the Regional pane chip (the component is
  reused inside the country row), the duplicate LED strip in
  `SitrepPanel`, the Forming fold (its two cards feed the primer).
- Door-in events keep working: `osint:set-pane` (`watch`/`regional`/
  legacy ids → Commands, with the right level opened), `watch:focus`
  (→ open that command's board card / that field's SITREP),
  `regional:select` (→ open that country's row).

### Decisions needed

1. **Commands is the landing; Watch and Regional retire into it; Feeds
   and Sources stay as chips** — recommended yes.

## 7. Tracking — what gets watched, and how it is set (2026-10-06, after the OSINT round)

> Not a walkthrough of a tab: a survey of every door through which a
> country or an airfield enters the app, prompted by "the settings are a
> little clunky — I want an elegant, streamlined way to make adjustments".

### What the survey found

An airfield could be tracked through **four lists in four editors** (Force
posture, METAR stations, SITREP bases, ★ must-tracks) with three id schemes
(`mp-`, free ids, ICAO-only), and a country through two. Each editor held
its own in-memory copy and the drawer saved all of them on every Save, so
an edit made anywhere else (a SITREP add, a map ★, an Apply) could be
reverted by an unrelated Preferences save made later from a stale drawer.
Three defects fell out of reading it:

- **T1 — Apply excluded rows the user never saw.** *Fix.* `materializedIds`
  recorded every id the derivation PRODUCED; a derived base a manual row
  already held was never written, read as "missing" at the next Apply, and
  was added to `excludedIds` for good.
- **T2 — Country strings never joined.** *Fix.* OurAirports gives ISO2
  ("JO"), the curated hubs "" , the catalogue "jordan", the feeds "Jordan"
  — every country-keyed join compared raw strings.
- **T3 — The drawer loaded once per session.** *Fix.* `if (!open || loaded)
  return;` — the second open posted the first open's lists. Also: the
  SITREP bases route wrote the shared row for any crew member; the Crisis
  map read `trackedLocations` off the `{ prefs }` envelope (always empty).
